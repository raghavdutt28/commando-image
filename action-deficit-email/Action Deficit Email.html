Action Deficit

/ˈak-shən ˈde-fə-sət/ · noun
 
 "You get alerts but no one can define action or completion."

A camera flags a blocked access route. A sensor records an exception. A dashboard turns red. Then the follow-up waits for a call, a forwarded message or the next shift.

Automation is moving quickly in all industries. IFR reports that India installed almost 10,500 industrial robots in 2025, up 15%. But installing more technology does not establish who will act on the issues it reveals.

Source: IFR · World Robotics 2026 release, 24 September 2026 ↗︎

The action deficit is the gap between issues identified and responses completed with evidence. Collecting data, interpreting it and getting the work done are separate responsibilities.

A documented example

At BP-Husky's Ohio refinery on 20 September 2022, 3,712 alarms were recorded over roughly 12 hours. The U.S. Chemical Safety Board also found that an email summarising an operating meeting was never sent, with no evidence that the agreed actions reached the incoming shift.

Source: U.S. Chemical Safety Board · BP-Husky safety release, 30 September 2026 ↗︎

The investigation involved several safety failures. Its lesson for this newsletter is specific: detection and a discussion do not establish an acknowledged, completed response.

An action is complete when someone checks the outcome via on-site proof.
